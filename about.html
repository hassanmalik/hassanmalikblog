I’m most interested in software architecture that has to earn trust after the demo ends.

I currently work in software and data engineering at Moderna, building production applications and data workflows for scientific teams. Earlier roles at Meta Reality Labs, Google Cloud, Google Shopping / Express and the State of California exposed me to a very different set of systems: hardware programs, global operations, logistics, public platforms and large cross-functional programs.

That range created a bias toward practical architecture. A technically elegant system still fails if it ignores workflow fit, permissions, failure recovery, operating ownership or the economics of adoption.

I write about the problems Staff and Principal engineers are expected to own: ambiguous problem framing, system boundaries, distributed failure modes, migrations, cross-team interfaces, technical tradeoffs and measurable outcomes. Applied AI is part of that picture, not a substitute for software engineering fundamentals.

My technical writing covers production AI, enterprise RAG, agentic systems, evaluation, scientific software and forward deployed engineering. I use reference architectures and explicit assumptions rather than presenting hypothetical designs as employer systems.

I also study the business side of technology through MBA coursework at Boston University, building on graduate training in electrical engineering.

What I write about

Four recurring questions.

01

How should AI fit into scientific work?

Not “where can we add an LLM?” but where intelligence, retrieval, tools, provenance, evaluation and human judgment create leverage.

02

How should production AI fail?

Retries, state ownership, idempotency, permission boundaries, partial execution and human escalation are architecture decisions.

03

What makes a field deployment reusable?

A strong FDE solves the customer problem while identifying the stable primitive that belongs in the platform—and the bespoke detail that does not.

04

How do technical leaders create leverage?

By making good decisions repeatable: clear architecture boundaries, design reviews, reusable interfaces, migration paths, operating mechanisms, mentoring, and alignment across teams.
