About

I like systems with consequences.

My work sits where software engineering, data, operational reality and technical decision-making collide.

Hassan Malik

Software + data engineering
AI systems
Biotech R&D

I’m most interested in engineering that has to earn trust after the demo ends.

That means software for scientific teams, AI capabilities embedded in real workflows, data products used to make operating decisions and platforms that have to survive organizational boundaries.

My background spans Moderna Therapeutics, Meta Reality Labs, Google Cloud, Google Shopping / Express and the State of California. That range created a bias toward practical architecture: good systems have to work technically, fit the workflow and survive the organization around them.

I also study the business side of technology through MBA coursework at Boston University, building on graduate training in electrical engineering.

What I write about

Four recurring questions.

01

How should AI fit into scientific work?

Not “where can we add an LLM?” but where intelligence, retrieval, tools, provenance, evaluation and human judgment create leverage.

02

Why do internal tools fail to get adopted?

Workflow fit, trust, latency, transparency, ownership and recovery paths are architectural concerns—not change-management footnotes.

03

What makes data operational?

Data becomes valuable when it closes a decision loop: signal, context, owner, action and feedback.

04

How do technical leaders span boundaries?

By being fluent enough in code, systems, operations, metrics, risk and business context to keep interfaces from becoming failure points.
